In October 2020 we adopted a rescue dog, a Galgo Espānol. And when you do that, you can quickly gain new friends and acquaintances, even if only on social media. After all, 2020!

So I did and I often exchange messages with a women who adopted 2 years ago and also lives in the Netherlands. We share many values around environmental protection, climate crisis and animal welfare.

So one of her posts on Instagram left me somewhat confused. She had a council employee call around to ask if she owned a dog. Dogs are taxed in the Netherlands. So this was a check. She proudly reported how silent and calm her dog remained so she could state no, she didn’t own a dog.

I would have filed that under everyday lies and petty tax avoidance. After all, we are quite happy to pay dog tax because our municipality provides good infrastructure and services for dog owners. But what got me a bit annoyed was that she complained in her previous post about Dutch people ignoring the fireworks ban. Again, I fully agree with her on fireworks, especially in the Netherlands where people are utterly obsessed with explosives. Fireworks are a needless source of animal trauma, catastrophic injuries, pollution and environmental damage… and 14 million Euros in insurance claims per year.

But the issue was she first called out people for ignoring the law to then proudly proclaim that she just ignored it, too.

We argued but stayed friends. And I think this doesn’t happen often enough so I cherish the relationship. And I happily read and engaged with her various posts on rescue dogs and veganism. Which got me wondering if she might have some advice as we are hoping to transition our dog to vegan food eventually. She posts pretty radical content about veganism. We are, on the other hand, _overwhelmingly vegan for the majority of time_. And particularly during a pandemic and resulting lock-downs, we had to sacrifice ideological purity for simple sanity. So we do eat climate positive eggs and we treat ourselves to the occasional, organic french soft cheese or Dutch vintage gouda due to the lack of convincing vegan alternatives.

So I thought she must be have something for us. A plan, a hint, some practical things to convince our dog that the vegan food we bought is edible.

But as it turns out, her veganism stops short of her dog who gets fresh meat in all sorts of elaborate combinations, combined with freshly prepared vegetables, an egg per week, but just the yolk. The list went on. And I decided to not feel bad about the fact that we just feed organic dry food from a bag and some snacks, including plant based options.

So why do I share this story of blatant value inconsistency and conscious blindspots? Because I realised that strict rules, loudly proclaimed will make you a hypocrite with every small misstep.

A positive, value-based system with enough flexibility for the unplanned (like pandemics) combined with solidarity and trying to stay clear of condemnation seems better for longterm happiness and your social media profile.
