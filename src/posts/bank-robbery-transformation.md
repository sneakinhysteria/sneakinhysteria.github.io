You can’t grow by running away. If you do it repeatedly, there is a high likelihood that the issue will follow you.

Solving issues requires a conscious examination of behaviour and a crystal clear definition of the world without the issue, in software engineering, this is often described as the fixed state, or expected behaviour. It’s called expected because it can be described without having been experienced or achieved.

Every transformation needs a destination, an expression of the desired end goal that your people can rally behind and put in the work for, anticipating the reward of the expected benefit. This destination should be a positive, attainable and reasonably concrete vision, so people can’t wiggle out of or superimpose their own agendas.

> Vague visions kill accountability.  
>   
> At my work, I often recognise a pattern of brands having a very detailed understanding of what they try to transform away from, but not nearly as often can they articulate what they want to transform into.

Digital transformation is not a getaway car to drive from the crime scene as quickly as possible, measuring the distance between you and the bank as a KPI, ignoring the heading.

Digital transformation is a planned trip with a clear start and end location and waypoints that are based on data. Reality may lead to diversions and route recalucations. And the exact destination may also change during the journey as a result of learning on the way.
